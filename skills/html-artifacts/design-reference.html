Streaming responses for /chat

The endpoint holds the connection until the model finishes, then returns one JSON blob — eleven seconds of spinner for a 600-token answer. Three implementations are viable. Server-Sent Events is the recommendation: first token in 90 ms without the connection lifecycle a WebSocket would force us to own. Two blocking review notes stand between the prototype and a mergeable PR.

Decision

Take B. The 5 ms it gives up to WebSockets is noise next to the two weeks of load-balancer work C needs, and cancel-by-second-call is a fine trade until interrupts are a real feature rather than a nice-to-have.

  1. 1
    BlockingUndefined req.signal

    Undefined on the Node adapter, so aborts never reach the model. A closed tab leaves the stream running to completion — the exact mechanism behind INC-2291.

  2. 2
    BlockingEmpty catch swallows everything

    .catch(() => {}) eats a model 429 as happily as a client disconnect. The reader sees a clean stream that stops mid-sentence, and we get no telemetry.

  3. 3
    NitListener never removed

    Harmless per request, but it will read as a false positive the next time the leak suite runs.

  4. 4
    QuestionBuffering header?

    Does SSE_HEADERS set X-Accel-Buffering: no? nginx buffers 4 KB by default — about three seconds of tokens, which erases the whole benefit of Option B.

What worked

The rollback path was one command and took twelve minutes end to end, including the canary check.

What didn't

Seventeen minutes between the latency cliff and the first human noticing, because nothing was watching the thing we had just changed.

Ship Option B

Fix the two blockers in pipeBackpressure, confirm the buffering header, then open the PR.
